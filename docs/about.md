# About DevCol

DevCol is short for "development collaboration." It's my one-person game development project, and this site is where I keep the record of it: what I build, what breaks, and what I learn from fixing it.

I'd rather show the work while it's happening than wait for a launch trailer. The DevLog has progress notes and debugging write-ups, including the mistakes. The Projects section collects the games and prototypes I've finished. Study holds shorter notes on Unreal Engine, C++, and tools that I wanted to remember. Answers to common questions are in the [FAQ](/faq).

## Who makes it

I'm In Goo Lee, and I go by **in9** online. I'm a game programmer based in Korea, working in Unreal Engine 5 and C++. Every post, project page, and wiki entry on this site is written by me, from my own project files, editor logs, and commits. When a page was made with AI assistance, it says so on that page; the [devcol.net project page](/projects/devcol-net) explains exactly how this site itself was built.

My background is a mix of software and games. I studied Management Information Systems at a university in the United States, which is also where my English comes from, and later went through a game development bootcamp in Korea. Since then I've been building games on my own: two Unity titles, [Press Plane](/projects/press-plane) and [Rock Paper Scissors - Advance](/projects/rock-paper-scissors-advance), are released on Google Play and playable in the browser, and a set of Unreal Engine prototypes, from a [5-day FPS](/projects/building-an-unreal-engine-5-fps-in-5-days) to the three-stage team project [LuckySeven](/projects/luckyseven-ue5-team-project), are documented under Projects. I'm currently in an Unreal Engine client developer course and spend most of my time on PSEUDO.

## What I'm building

**PSEUDO** is a stylish action RPG in Unreal Engine 5 where a 2d6 dice roll decides the outcome of your skills, with a multiplayer arena mode. It's built in C++ on the Gameplay Ability System with server-authoritative replication, and the [PSEUDO DevLog](/blog/pseudo) follows it bug by bug: prediction, hit detection, MetaHuman and Paragon character assembly, and the rest. The [Wiki](/wiki/) documents the game's systems as they're designed.

Before PSEUDO I built **Tumble Teams**, a multiplayer warm-up project that taught me most of what I know about listen servers, GAS attributes, and replicated chat. Its notes are on the [Tumble Teams](/blog/tumble-teams) page. On the side there are the **Arcade Web** games, small browser games like [Stack Up](/projects/stack-up) and [Bug Squash](/projects/bug-squash) that double as a testbed for the site's analytics, and a few AI-assisted desktop tools.

## What you'll find here

- **DevLog**: dated write-ups of real problems, what I ruled out, and the fix. If a post is about a bug, it includes the symptom, the repro, and the root cause, so the next person searching for the same error message can skip the dead ends.
- **Projects**: one page per finished game or prototype, with screenshots, what it does, and what I'd do differently.
- **Study**: shorter reference notes on Unreal Engine, C++, Git, and tooling.
- **Wiki**: the design documents for PSEUDO's systems.

## Why in public

Writing the fix down is the only way I reliably remember it, and publishing it means someone else might find it before they spend a day on the same problem. It also keeps me honest about progress: a dated DevLog is harder to fudge than a roadmap. Feedback from people who've played something here or hit the same bug is the main thing I'm after.

If you've played something here or want to talk about it, the [Discord](https://discord.gg/7YTgpP8CCt) is the quickest way to reach me. There's also [X](https://x.com/DevCol_Main), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@devcol), [GitHub](https://github.com/devcol-main), and the [Contact](/contact) page.
