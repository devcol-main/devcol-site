# World and Tone

This page is about what kind of story PSEUDO tells and how it should feel. It stays on the near side of the story on purpose: no twists, no ending, nothing past the setup a player sees in the first hour.

## The premise

PSEUDO has two characters, and they live in two different places.

- **The main character (MC)** lives in an ordinary near-future world. AI took his job. With his days suddenly empty, he spends them logging in to a game.
- **The sub character (SC)** is the avatar he logs in as. Where the MC is a man with nothing to do, the SC is built for action: a gunslinger who fights with a greatsword as readily as with two pistols. The job motif behind him is the **Gambler**, which is where the dice come from.

You play both. In the **hub**, you're the MC: a small world rather than an open one, with a place to decorate, a shop, portals into the stages, and a portal for re-challenging bosses you've already beaten. It starts sparse and fills in as you progress, with new functions unlocking and trophies from bosses taking up the display slots. In the **stages**, you're the SC, and the game becomes a stylish action RPG built on [2d6 dice](/wiki/systems/dice).

The split is not just a menu with a character attached. The hub is slow and quiet; the stages are fast and loud. Going from one to the other is the core rhythm of the game, and the distance between the two places is part of what the story is about.

## The questions

PSEUDO started from a list of questions I kept returning to, and they all ended up folded into one:

> **Will you still roll?**

Under it sit the earlier ones:

- **Work and meaning.** If an AI can produce the result, does doing it yourself still mean anything? This is the MC's situation, and it stays in the background. The game doesn't lecture about AI. It just puts a man in that position and lets the rest of the game happen around him.
- **Chance and freedom.** Is the world a matter of chance? What does a choice mean when the odds are fixed?
- **Luck and effort.** Luck matters. Does effort matter more? The dice system is built on exactly this line: [skill earns the roll, luck decides the result](/wiki/systems/dice).
- **Simulation.** If the world is computed, does that change anything?

The design goal is to **ask, not answer**. PSEUDO doesn't have a lesson. It has a question it puts to the player through play, and it's careful not to mark any response as the correct one.

## References

Three works sit behind the tone:

- **The Butterfly Dream (Zhuangzi).** Zhuangzi dreams he is a butterfly, wakes, and can't tell whether he is a man who dreamed of being a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming of being a man. The butterfly is the game's central motif because of this story. See [Butterflies: The Signal System](/wiki/lore/butterfly-signals).
- **The Matrix.** A world that runs on rules someone else wrote, and small wrongnesses that give it away.
- **Inception.** Layers of reality and the objects people carry to tell them apart.

None of these is quoted or imitated directly. They set the mood: unease that arrives quietly, through details, rather than through exposition.

## The casino, and the shape of luck

The game has three kinds of places with three relationships to luck:

| Place | What decides the outcome |
|---|---|
| Stages | Skill and luck together. Good play earns dice; dice add swing. |
| Casino (planned, in the hub) | Luck alone. Stake in-game currency, win or lose it. |
| Arena (multiplayer) | Everyone rolls each round to change the rules for everyone. |

The casino exists as a contrast. Next to the stages, where effort keeps buying you more chances, a room where nothing you do matters is supposed to feel hollow, not exciting. It never touches real money or paid currency, and nothing in the game does.

## Art direction

- **Realism from market assets**, in the range of *Stellar Blade*: grounded materials, cinematic lighting, characters that look like people. The SC is built from a Paragon character with a MetaHuman face; the MC is a full MetaHuman.
- **Stylish action**: hits that land with weight, finishers, and a slow-motion dice roll that makes every roll an event.
- **Mature content by choice.** Blood effects and decals, finisher animations, and in the zombie stage, dismemberment. The rating target is adult on purpose; the dice alone wouldn't put it there.
- **Restrained brand colors.** The logo, menus and HUD frame use one or two colors plus neutrals. Strong color is saved for moments that mean something (see the butterflies).
- **Symbols, not creatures.** The animals and marks that carry meaning appear as thin, glowing line art layered over the realistic world, not as 3D animals. It's cheaper, it's consistent, and the contrast between realistic world and drawn symbol does part of the work.

## The title

**PSEUDO** is *pseudo-random* and *pseudo-reality* at once. The logo is written **PSEUd0**: an uppercase word with a lowercase `d` (dice notation) and a slashed zero, so the zero can never be mistaken for an O. A thin line-art butterfly with dice pips on its wings sits beside it.

On the title screen the logo glitches at random intervals: letters slip out of alignment for a moment and settle back. It's quiet and infrequent. The title screen's only line of text is the question above.

On a store page or in search, the name is always plain **PSEUDO**. The `PSEUd0` spelling is for the logo only.

## Tone in one line

Calm on the surface, wrong in small ways, and asking something it won't answer for you.
